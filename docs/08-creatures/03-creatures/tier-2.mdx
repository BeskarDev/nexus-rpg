### Orc Raider

<CreatureLore>

An orc down from the dry country at harvest, as their band has come down every harvest anyone can remember. They are not an invasion and they do not want the land. They want the grain, and they intend to be home before the season turns.

### Clay Servant

<CreatureLore>

A knee-high figure of fired clay with a hoe moulded into its fists, buried by the hundred so the dead would not have to work their own fields. The potters who made them wrote the work into the clay before the firing, in an age that knew how. The potters are long gone and the figures are still working.

<LoreSection label="Ecology"><LoreTag>Desert</LoreTag><LoreTag>Hills</LoreTag><LoreTag>Necropolis</LoreTag><LoreTag>Ruins</LoreTag><LoreTag>Tomb</LoreTag><LoreTag>Vault</LoreTag> They do not eat, wear out, or stop. The tombs of the old dynasties were buried with a chamber of them, ranked by the hundred and moulded for one task each: the diggers, the water carriers, the ones with a loom. Nobody has made one since. The work is written into a fired core, and the cutting of cores was Ghahar craft that went into the ground with the fortresses that held it, so a tomb furnished today gets painted figures that will stand in the dark forever and never lift a hoe. Ghahar smiths still raise new servants, and every one of them is built around a core dug out of somewhere older, which is most of why the old chambers get opened at all. The rule the potters worked to is still repeated by the people who set the figures out, that one moulded with its face turned from the door will not serve, so the faces all look at whoever comes in. What wakes them is the chamber being disturbed, and they go back to standing when it is quiet again.</LoreSection>

A thin, quick eater of the dead, at home in the necropolis and in the graves the robbers opened first. They talk, in a fashion, and it has never once been a conversation. The grave-wardens of the old cities know which colonies are theirs and leave a body out at the end of the season.

### Goblin Chief

<CreatureLore>

The one who chose this bend in the road, told everyone where to stand, and has already decided which way they personally are leaving. They hold the gang by being right about which carts are worth stopping.

<LoreSection label="Ecology"><LoreTag>Desert</LoreTag><LoreTag>Grassland</LoreTag><LoreTag>Hills</LoreTag><LoreTag>Road</LoreTag><LoreTag>Ruins</LoreTag><LoreTag>Caves</LoreTag><LoreTag>Den</LoreTag> A ringleader holds a gang by judgement rather than by strength, and most of them are the smallest thing in it. They know which stretch of road is watched, which villages will pay to be left alone, and which drover will hand over half a load rather than lose a wheel. Some of those arrangements run for years and are renewed every season. A gang that loses their ringleader scatters within the week and turns up on somebody else's road. The ones who last are the ones who never let a robbery become a fight.</LoreSection>

<LoreSection label="Tactics">They start the fight and then get out of it, running the gang from the back with the lash reaching over the top of them. **Get In Front** puts whoever is beside them in the way of anything aimed at them, so keep a skulker within arm's reach at all times and treat the gang as their armor. The lash is for anyone unarmored and the javelins for anyone who is not, and they will spend a whole fight at range if the ground allows. They compete for one Quick Action between **Get In Front** and **Quick Escape**, so once the goblins beside them are down the escape is all they have left. Once they are Wounded, **Spring the Trap** brings two more goblins down off the rocks and shifts everyone already fighting into a better position, so fire it before the gang is spent rather than after. **They can be bought before that and not after.** Take the toll and they wave the party through, and the deal holds only as long as the party keeps travelling that road.</LoreSection>
