### Mummy

<CreatureLore>

A body that was wrapped, oiled and packed with resin by people who were paid well to do it properly. They lie in the chamber cut for them, with their name on the wall and their jars at their feet, and they have never once stopped expecting to be treated as what they were. What wakes them is the door.

### Edimmu Shade

<CreatureLore>

Somebody who died where nobody found them and stayed there. They come up out of the ground they fell on, thin and grey and cold enough to feel before they touch anybody, and they go straight for the nearest living warmth. ### Kusarikku

<CreatureLore>

A horned figure with a human chest and arms and the legs of a bull, standing a head and a half above anybody who comes to the door. They are cut from stone and set in a pair, one either side of a gateway, and once they are standing they never sit down again. They are not one of the Minotaur folk and never were: no name, no herd and no people.

<LoreSection label="Ecology"><LoreTag>Desert</LoreTag><LoreTag>Hills</LoreTag><LoreTag>Ruins</LoreTag><LoreTag>Temple</LoreTag><LoreTag>City</LoreTag><LoreTag>Necropolis</LoreTag><LoreTag>Tomb</LoreTag> They do not eat and they do not sleep, and there is nothing in them that wants anything except the doorway they were given. That is why a Kusarikku will put somebody outside and then stand there while the same person walks back up to the door. A person is not what they were set against. The tomb-cutters hold that the ward is the creature and not the stone, so a gateway whose pair has been broken is a gateway standing open, whatever the door itself is doing, and whatever was on the other side has been waiting the whole time. Cities that inherit an old gate with both figures still on it tend to route the new road around it.</LoreSection>

### Elder Ghoul

<CreatureLore>

The one at the bottom of a colony that has held the same burial ground for a long time. Nothing down there eats until the elder has. They are bigger than the rest and slower to be drawn off a body, and they have words and use them, mostly to keep their own kind off whatever they are working on.

The grave-wardens who work a colony's ground can tell from the ordinary ghouls whether there is an elder under them, and the cities that can afford to will have a season's dead delivered rather than find out the other way.
