### Veteran

<CreatureLore>

A soldier past forty, taken on at sixteen and still standing in the line at an age when most of the file has gone home to a plot of land. ### Ogre

<CreatureLore>

Three metres tall and always hungry, with a tree for a club and a shoulder like a wall. Ogres are the smallest of the giants and the only ones that live where people do, out on the bad ground that no city has ever bothered to claim. They will take a price and stand guard for a season, which has convinced a great many people that an ogre is somebody you can come to an arrangement with. The arrangement lasts exactly as long as the ogre is fed.

<LoreSection label="Ecology"><LoreTag>Hills</LoreTag><LoreTag>Grassland</LoreTag><LoreTag>Marsh</LoreTag><LoreTag>Farmland</LoreTag><LoreTag>Road</LoreTag><LoreTag>Caves</LoreTag><LoreTag>Lair</LoreTag> They eat constantly and they are not particular. An ogre works a stretch of hill or marsh the way a bear does, walking the same round every few days, taking whatever is slow, and coming down onto the farmland when the round runs thin. They know that people carry food and they have learned that people are food. Ogres are the only giants that deal with anyone. They keep to twos and threes, they know enough trade-speech to name a price, and a village will pay them in cattle to be left alone while a raiding band hires one for a season. None of that makes them a people. An ogre keeps no custom and remembers no agreement past the day it fed them, so a bargain holds while it is the easiest food in reach and breaks the hour it is not, without warning. Everybody who deals with an ogre knows this and does it anyway, because the alternative is having the ogre come to them. The priests say the giants came out of the titans and have never forgiven the world for what came after. An ogre asked about it says nothing that settles the question.</LoreSection>

### Cult Priest

<CreatureLore>

A priest of a fringe death-cult, keeping rites the orthodox temples struck off their calendars. They meet at night in a necropolis nobody has swept in years, in a robe that smells of the censer, with a mark of their cult cut into everything they own. What they offer the people who come down to them is that death is a power worth speaking to, and that they know how to speak to it.

<LoreSection label="Ecology"><LoreTag>Desert</LoreTag><LoreTag>Grassland</LoreTag><LoreTag>Necropolis</LoreTag><LoreTag>Ruins</LoreTag><LoreTag>Crypt</LoreTag><LoreTag>Shrine</LoreTag> They are not a temple and they do not have one. A cult like this is nine or ten people who meet in a crypt on the nights the calendar says nothing happens, and the priest is whoever learned the words from the last one. They sit with the dying the temples will not sit with, and they lay out the dead of families who cannot pay for a proper burial, and they do the work for nothing. That is most of how they grow. The orthodox priesthoods call the rites heresy and the city watches call them a nuisance, and neither of them goes into the necropolis after dark to do anything about it. Where a colony of ghouls holds ground the cult wants, the cult feeds them exactly as the grave-wardens do and tells itself the prayer is the difference. Some say it is one of their rites that leaves a body walking instead of resting. The cult says those are the rites somebody interrupted.</LoreSection>

### Captain

<CreatureLore>

An officer of a city or a warlord, appointed out of a landholding family and handed a company to keep alive. They wear the same leather as their soldiers and carry a sword instead of a spear, which is how everyone on the field knows which one they are. They have never had to fight in the front rank.

<LoreSection label="Ecology"><LoreTag>Grassland</LoreTag><LoreTag>River</LoreTag><LoreTag>Desert</LoreTag><LoreTag>City</LoreTag><LoreTag>Fortress</LoreTag><LoreTag>Road</LoreTag> A captain is appointed rather than promoted, usually out of a family that holds land near the city and can be held to account if the company goes wrong. They draw ten times a soldier's pay, eat better than their soldiers, and are expected to spend some of the difference on them. Most of the work is not fighting. They count the muster, sign for the grain, walk the wall at night, and settle whatever their soldiers have done in the wineshops. Both sides know an officer is worth more alive than dead, so a captain taken in the field is held for ransom and the letters go back and forth for a season. Their own soldiers know it too, and a file that believes its captain would sell them does not stand.</LoreSection>

<LoreSection label="Physiology"> Depends on the folk of this creature. See "Physical Traits by Folk" in the Folk section for more details.</LoreSection>

<LoreSection label="Tactics">They fight from inside their own file rather than in front of it. Their one Quick Action goes to **Dress the Line** for an ally under attack and to **Set the Shield** when the attention turns on them. They take whoever has broken into the formation with the **Longsword**, and use **Shield Bash** to push someone off an ally and follow them. After **Hold the Line** they look for two enemies in reach at once. They do not chase and they do not rout, and cornered with the file gone they offer a ransom.</LoreSection>
