### Jackal

<CreatureLore>

A lean grey scavenger of the burial roads. Jackals follow funerals at the edge of a thrown stone's range, and they have learned which funerals leave food. Every gravedigger knows the ones that work their stretch, and none of them has ever driven one off for good.

<LoreSection label="Ecology"><LoreTag>Desert</LoreTag><LoreTag>Grassland</LoreTag><LoreTag>Road</LoreTag><LoreTag>Necropolis</LoreTag> They eat what is left. Offerings, grave goods with fat in them, animals that died on the road, whatever a caravan drops. They range in family groups of four to nine and hunt only what is already dying. A pack learns a funeral's route within a season and will be at the graveside before the mourners. Some cities take that as the dead being met on the road. Others take it as a warning that the rites were begun badly, and start again.</LoreSection>

<LoreSection label="Physiology"><LoreTag>45 cm at the shoulder, 1.1 m nose to tail</LoreTag><LoreTag>9 kg</LoreTag><LoreTag>8 to 10 years</LoreTag> A mated pair keeps to each other for life and raises one litter of four to six pups a year in a dug den. The pups run with the pack by the end of their first season, and most of a litter does not see a second one.</LoreSection>

<LoreSection label="Tactics">They will not open a fight. They follow, wait for someone to fall behind, and take the smallest or the slowest. In a fight they circle rather than close. **Pack Tactics** pays only when two of them reach the same person, so run them in pairs on one target and have them give up on anyone they cannot get behind. A strong bite puts anyone of their own size or smaller prone, so send the next jackal at whoever is already down. They break off the moment it costs them anything, and one dead jackal is usually enough. They will not follow anyone who turns and advances, and they fall back to the edge of the light and wait.</LoreSection>

### Leatherwing

<CreatureLore>

A pigeon-sized flier, all leather wing and beaked snout, at home in harbour cities for as long as there have been harbour cities. They sit on market awnings, nest in roof beams, and take bread out of baskets nobody is watching. Sailors say a quay without leatherwings is a quay the sea has turned against, and no captain who believes it will tie up there.

<LoreSection label="Ecology"><LoreTag>Coast</LoreTag><LoreTag>Sea</LoreTag><LoreTag>City</LoreTag> They eat scraps, fish guts, dropped bread, and insects. A market with leatherwings has fewer rats than a market without. Everyone who keeps records knows this. Nobody says it in a council meeting. Flocks follow the boats in and strip the guts off the water before the catch is landed. Wild colonies nest on sea cliffs. The city flocks are a separate population, and nobody brought them in. They moved into the cities as the cities grew. A trained one carries messages better than a pigeon, because a leatherwing remembers roofs and coastlines instead of a single home loft. It takes most of a year to train one, and the trainers are a small and well-paid trade.</LoreSection>

<LoreSection label="Physiology"><LoreTag>35 cm long, 90 cm wingspan</LoreTag><LoreTag>600 g</LoreTag><LoreTag>15 to 20 years</LoreTag> A pair holds one nest site for years and raises two eggs at a time, twice a year, on a cliff ledge or a roof beam. Chicks fly at ten weeks and stay with the flock they hatched in. A city colony is mostly one extended family, which is part of why they all know the same faces.</LoreSection>

<LoreSection label="Tactics">Do not run them as a fight. What they do is scream. A flock that does not recognise somebody raises a racket that carries across a whole market, and while the flock is up the district is awake and watching. They remember faces, so anyone who has fed one is not a stranger to the flock for days. Use **Snatching Beak** on what is held rather than what is guarded, then go straight up to a roofline nothing can follow to. They break off by climbing out of reach, and they come back.</LoreSection>

### Reed Viper

<CreatureLore>

A forearm-long snake the colour of dry reed. They live in the irrigation channels, and the farmers who work those channels know the sound of one leaving. They are not aggressive. They are just always lying where somebody is about to step.

<LoreSection label="Ecology"><LoreTag>River</LoreTag><LoreTag>Marsh</LoreTag><LoreTag>Farmland</LoreTag> They hunt rats and frogs along the channel banks. They spend the heat of the day under matting, stacked reed, or in the shade of the bank. Harvest is when people meet them, because harvest is when people walk the channels barefoot. Villages do not kill them. They keep the rats down, and any that are killed are replaced within a season.</LoreSection>

<LoreSection label="Physiology"><LoreTag>35 cm long</LoreTag><LoreTag>200 g</LoreTag><LoreTag>6 to 8 years</LoreTag> They lay six to ten eggs under warm matting or a stacked reed pile at the end of summer and leave them there. Hatchlings are venomous from the first day and a finger's length long, which is how a village ends up with a bad stretch of channel in a single season.</LoreSection>

<LoreSection label="Tactics">They strike once, from cover, at whoever puts a foot near them. **Strike from Cover** only works while they are unseen, so open from hiding and do not open twice. After the strike they leave for the water and they will not pursue. The bite is not what kills. Run the poison in somebody who is days from a healer.</LoreSection>

### Tomb Beetles

<CreatureLore>

Finger-long scarabs that live on grave goods, funeral tallow, and the dead themselves. The priests of the necropolis call them the last of the offerings, left to eat what the living stopped bringing. Disturb the floor and they come up through it.

<LoreSection label="Ecology"><LoreTag>Desert</LoreTag><LoreTag>Ruins</LoreTag><LoreTag>Necropolis</LoreTag><LoreTag>Tomb</LoreTag> A colony lives in the packed earth under a tomb floor. They eat the fat in funeral lamps, the leather and linen of grave goods, and anything organic left as an offering. They do not hunt. They boil up when the floor above them is walked on, worked, or broken. They do not lie in wait, so whoever wakes them has just broken the floor they nest under, and that is usually a robber, a mason, or a priest come to re-seal a chamber. A tomb with intact floors and no beetles has usually been emptied already.</LoreSection>

<LoreSection label="Physiology"><LoreTag>6 cm long</LoreTag><LoreTag>20 g</LoreTag><LoreTag>4 years</LoreTag> Eggs go into the packed earth in their hundreds, and the grubs feed underground for two years on whatever is buried above them. That is why a broken floor brings up a generation rather than a handful. A swarm is two to three thousand of them, and the colony they come from is as old as the tomb.</LoreSection>

<LoreSection label="Tactics">They occupy an area and threaten only what stands in it, so **Devouring Bites** hits everyone who stayed. Fire drives them off and they will not cross it. A torch swept low, a lamp broken on the floor, a burning cloak dropped in a doorway, any of it turns them. They will not leave the colony's ground, and that ground is the whole chamber floor rather than one corner of it, so backing out of the room gives it up rather than winning it. Once it is empty they settle back into the floor.</LoreSection>
