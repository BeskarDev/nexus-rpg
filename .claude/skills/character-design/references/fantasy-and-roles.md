# Player Fantasy and Party Roles — steps 1 and 2

## Step 1 — Name the fantasy

Write **one sentence** in the player's voice, not the designer's:

> *"I want to be the shield that holds the line while my friends do the killing."*
> *"I want to be the one who knows what the omens mean before anyone else does."*
> *"I want to fight with my hands and be better at it than people with swords."*

Rules for the sentence:

- **It names a verb, not a noun.** "A priest" is a job title; "the one who drags people back
  from the edge" is a fantasy. The verb is what the build has to be able to DO at rank 1.
- **It is specific enough to exclude things.** If the sentence would fit six of the
  twenty-five archetypes, it is not finished. Two Fighting archetypes that both say "I hit
  things well" have no reason to both exist.
- **It survives contact with the rules.** "I want to be untouchable" is not buildable at rank
  1; "I want to be the hardest target in the party" is (Dodge, light armor, Evade).

Everything downstream is checked against this sentence. When a choice cannot be traced to it,
the choice is wrong or the sentence was.

### The three questions that expose a weak fantasy

1. **What does the character do on turn one of a fight?** Name the Action and the Quick Action.
2. **What do they do when there is no fight?** A quickstart character with nothing outside
   combat is half a character.
3. **What can they do that the party's other members cannot?** If the answer is "the same
   thing but slightly better", the fantasy is a duplicate.

## Step 2 — Judge the roles

Roles are written on the archetype as `Primary / Qualifier` — for example `Tank / Striker`,
`Support / Utility`. The **primary role is one of four party jobs**; the qualifier is either a
second party job or one of two play descriptors.

### The four party jobs

| Role | The promise | What the sheet must show at rank 1 |
|------|-------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Tank** | *"Hit me instead."* | High Parry or AV, plus a reason the enemy cannot simply walk past — a mark, a punish, a body-blocking effect, or an ally-protecting Quick Action |
| **Striker** | *"That one dies."* | A reliable damage line: a weapon or spell the build can use every turn, plus one way to make a hit land harder (art, talent rider, boon source) |
| **Support** | *"You are better because I am here."* | At least one ability that touches an ALLY — healing, a boon, a re-roll, an action given back, a condition removed |
| **Controller** | *"You do not get to do that."* | A way to impose a condition or deny ground: grapple, restrain, frighten, slow, difficult terrain, a zone |

**A role is a promise, and the promise is checked at rank 1.** The commonest audit failure is
a build that would be a fine Tank at rank 3 and holds nothing at rank 1.

### The two qualifiers

| Qualifier | Means |
|-----------|-------|
| **Utility** | The build's second half is out-of-combat: knowledge, crafting, medicine, tracking, infiltration. It buys the party options rather than outcomes in a fight. |
| **Hybrid** | The build genuinely splits its rank-1 budget between two systems (typically weapon + magic), and is deliberately weaker at each than a specialist. Rare and expensive — one archetype uses it. |

A qualifier is **not** a filter on the overview table and never stands alone: every archetype
leads with one of the four jobs.

### Choosing the pair

- **The primary role is what the fantasy's verb does.** "Holds the line" is Tank; "knows the
  omens" is Support (it gives the party information it did not have); "hits harder than people
  with swords" is Striker.
- **The qualifier is what the build does with its third rank-1 skill.** A Fighting/Athletics/
  Fortitude build is Tank / Striker; a Fighting/Nature/Insight build is Tank / Utility.
- **Two combat jobs means the build must actually do both on the same turn** — Charge (move +
  shove + attack) is genuinely Striker/Controller; a build that could attack OR grapple, one
  per turn, is a Striker with a fallback.

### Role coverage across the twenty-five

The published spread leans Striker-heavy, which is normal for a starter set (players pick
damage). When adding or revising an archetype, prefer the role the set is thin on over another
Striker — and never move an archetype's role to fix a table balance problem. The role follows
the build; if the role is wrong, the BUILD changes.

### Party-fit test

Imagine the character at a table with three others. Ask:

- If this build is missing, what does the party lose? (If nothing: the fantasy is a duplicate.)
- If the party already has two of this role, does this build still have a distinct job?
- Does the build need a specific ally to function? A rank-1 character may not be a
  combination piece — it must work in a party of strangers.
